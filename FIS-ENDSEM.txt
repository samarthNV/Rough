2. Introduction
In the ever-evolving landscape of financial services, ensuring robust software quality and seamless test management is paramount. As organizations transition between platforms, streamline processes, and deal with increased transaction volumes, there arises a need for scalable, automated solutions that can adapt quickly and efficiently. My internship at FIS Global as an IT Trainee Intern was situated within this dynamic context, focusing on enhancing the testing and automation capabilities of the TT2.0 project—a strategic initiative to streamline test execution, data transformation, and performance validation processes across multiple systems.

The core objective of my work revolved around solving challenges caused by inconsistent testcase structures and platform migrations. As the organization moved from Zephyr to TestRail and eventually to Zephyr Scale, maintaining a unified and aligned repository of testcases became crucial. This project aimed to bring automation, scalability, and performance reliability into the testing ecosystem through well-designed scripts and optimized CI/CD practices.

2.1 Significance of Test Automation in Financial Systems
In large financial institutions like FIS Global, the accuracy and resilience of test infrastructures are essential due to the sensitive nature of financial transactions and the scale at which they operate. A single testcase misalignment or performance bottleneck can cascade into significant operational delays and inconsistencies.

Automating the transformation and alignment of testcases not only eliminates manual effort but also ensures consistency across the testing life cycle. It reduces the risk of human error, enhances traceability, and supports rapid deployment in a continuously integrated environment like Jenkins. Furthermore, bulk automation—especially in testcase identification and classification—becomes vital when testcases number in the thousands.

2.2 Challenges in Testcase Management and Migration
During my internship, one of the major pain points I addressed was the misalignment of testcases due to multiple transitions between test management platforms. Legacy IDs from Zephyr, interim migrations to TestRail, and the final shift to Zephyr Scale had created a fragmented and inconsistent testcase ecosystem.

Manually transforming 3,000+ testcases and their associated data was not scalable. Adding to this complexity was the need to ensure that every CSV file—used for storing testcase data—was correctly formatted, renamed, and mapped to its corresponding script.

2.2.1 Technical Complexities and Integration Bottlenecks
Several layers of complexity emerged during script integration and performance optimization:

CI/CD Synchronization: Frequent Jenkins failures required extensive debugging and repeated integration cycles.

Cross-Platform Consistency: Aligning legacy testcase IDs across three different platforms involved intricate logic for mapping and content transformation.

Scalability: The Groovy scripts needed to be adaptable and reusable by different teams across FIS for future migrations and updates.

Performance Assurance: With tariff-driven spikes in transaction volumes, performance testing scripts were developed and integrated to test the platform’s ability to handle increased load.

2.2.2 Objectives of the Internship Project
The primary goals of my internship were:

To automate the alignment and transformation of testcases and their corresponding CSVs using Groovy.

To integrate performance testing into the existing pipeline to ensure robustness during high transaction periods.

To identify automated testcases programmatically and mark them in Zephyr Scale in bulk.

To gain practical exposure to tools and technologies such as Groovy, Maven, Jenkins, Bitbucket, Git, and Zephyr Scale.

To contribute creatively and collaboratively to the Innovatein48 Hackathon, showcasing cross-functional capabilities in a high-paced, innovation-driven environment.



3. Previous Work / Existing System Study
3.1 Existing Systems, Tools, and Approaches
In the domain of enterprise-level software quality assurance and test management, a variety of tools and methods have emerged to support test planning, execution, reporting, and automation. Within FIS Global, especially in large-scale projects like TT2.0, maintaining consistency and reliability across thousands of testcases poses significant challenges. Prior to the improvements implemented during this internship, several systems and practices were in place:

Zephyr and TestRail: These test management tools were successively used across different teams. Each provided basic features for test planning, execution, and traceability. However, inconsistencies arose due to frequent migrations and partial integrations.

Manual Testcase Transformation: Much of the testcase alignment process was handled manually, leading to duplication, mismatched mappings, and excessive time consumption.

Jenkins Pipelines: Jenkins was used for running CI/CD pipelines. While powerful, these pipelines occasionally failed due to insufficient script optimization or inadequate handling of legacy data formats.

Groovy Scripting (Ad Hoc Use): Prior scripting efforts in Groovy existed but were not modular, optimized, or generalized for broader team usage.

These systems, while individually functional, were fragmented and inefficient when combined, especially at the scale required for TT2.0.

3.2 Drawbacks of Existing Systems
Despite their capabilities, the existing test management and automation systems had several limitations:

Manual Overhead: Converting, aligning, and mapping thousands of testcases manually introduced inconsistencies, consumed significant engineering time, and was prone to human error.

Data Fragmentation: Migrations across platforms (Zephyr → TestRail → Zephyr Scale) resulted in broken ID references, disconnected CSVs, and duplicated scripts.

Pipeline Fragility: Jenkins pipelines frequently failed during builds due to dependencies on outdated scripts or misaligned test data formats.

Lack of Bulk Automation Support: There was no unified, reusable script to automate the transformation of CSVs and testcases at scale.

Limited Reusability and Modularity: Existing scripts were rigid and project-specific, preventing broader use by other QA teams.

These limitations highlighted the need for a unified, automated, and performance-optimized approach to testcase management, particularly within the evolving scope of TT2.0.

3.3 Problem Definition
This project aimed to address the systemic inefficiencies in testcase transformation, automation, and performance validation. Specifically, the internship focused on solving the following challenges:

Automated Testcase Transformation: Developing a robust Groovy script to align and transform over 3,000 testcases and CSV files, standardizing format and structure for future automation pipelines.

Performance Testing Integration: Creating a script to simulate load testing during tariff updates and high-volume transaction scenarios, ensuring TT2.0 could handle increased demands.

Bulk Identification and Classification: Writing a script to automatically detect and tag automated testcases in Zephyr Scale, enhancing test visibility and execution readiness.

Cross-Platform Mapping Logic: Addressing inconsistencies created by the migration between Zephyr, TestRail, and Zephyr Scale, through ID reconciliation and CSV alignment.

Pipeline Optimization: Enhancing Jenkins pipeline reliability by modularizing and error-proofing script executions.

By tackling these core issues, the project sought to create a scalable, maintainable, and efficient framework for test management and automation within FIS Global’s QA ecosystem.

4. Proposed System
The proposed system is a modular automation and performance-testing framework aimed at enhancing the quality assurance lifecycle in the TT2.0 platform. It focuses on automating large-scale testcase transformation, classifying test metadata for effective management, and ensuring scalability and reliability through performance benchmarking. The solution includes two core operational pipelines: the Testcase Transformation Flow and the Performance Validation Flow, supported by a centralized Test Classification Module.

4.1 Solution Overview
The architecture is structured around two automation flows designed to solve distinct problems in the QA cycle:

The Testcase Transformation Flow automates restructuring of testcases and CSV files into TT2.0-compliant formats, reducing manual effort and errors during migration.

The Performance Validation Flow performs scripted benchmarking of transaction-heavy features during high-load events, such as tariff updates.

Both flows are coordinated through a Test Classification & Management Module, which communicates with Zephyr Scale APIs to manage testcase metadata, classify test types (automated/manual), and reduce redundancy through intelligent tagging.

4.1.1 Testcase Transformation Flow
This flow automates the alignment of outdated or misaligned testcases with the updated test architecture:

Groovy scripts read and process raw testcase files and their corresponding CSV datasets.

Data alignment logic maps historic IDs to TT2.0-standard test identifiers using rule-based validation.

Parsed testcases are batch-uploaded into Zephyr Scale through API integration.

Output includes a clean JSON/CSV set of transformed testcases that can be integrated into Jenkins pipelines or test repositories.

This flow ensures that outdated or inconsistent QA assets are migrated into the new ecosystem without introducing errors, saving several hours of manual cleanup.

4.1.2 Performance Validation Flow
This flow facilitates continuous performance monitoring and load testing for core modules of TT2.0:

Transaction-heavy modules (e.g., tariff-based billing) are simulated through Groovy-based scripts under concurrent user conditions.

Output metrics such as response time, latency spikes, and failure rates are logged in real-time.

Performance baselines are compared with historical benchmarks to flag regressions.

Results are integrated into Jenkins reports to automate performance gate decisions in CI/CD.

This flow provides confidence in system reliability during peak-load conditions and validates production-readiness at scale.

4.1.3 Test Classification & Management Module
This supporting module ensures optimal organization and searchability of test artifacts:

Scripts classify testcases into "Manual", "Automated", or "Partial Automation" using pattern recognition in file structure and naming conventions.

Bulk tagging is performed in Zephyr Scale to improve test navigation and dashboard clarity.

Duplicates or redundant entries are detected and flagged to prevent repository bloat.

The module ensures the test management system remains lean, relevant, and developer/QE-friendly.

The system is designed with modular components and clear workflows to ensure scalability and adaptability to future changes. The key components of the framework, from testcase transformation to performance validation, interact seamlessly to ensure high-quality outputs while keeping the system flexible for future enhancements.

4.4.1 Model Components and Specifications

This approach automates error-prone manual updates, improving consistency and data integrity across test repositories.

Zephyr Scale for Testcase Classification: Once transformed, testcases are automatically classified in Zephyr Scale into automated or manual categories. The test classification model employs predefined rules to flag redundant or duplicate test entries.
